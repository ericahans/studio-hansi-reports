Bands from 2 to 5 have small samples (3–20 ads each) — noisy, so we can't say conversion truly worsens above 2. The real, consistent jump happens at the 1.5 threshold.

Why it matters Below 1.5 impressions per person (279 ads — the majority of the account), Barbie barely sells: CPA €121, nearly 3x worse than the account average. Once it crosses 1.5, conversion jumps ~6x and CPA drops to €42 — close to the best level of the whole month. It's the same pattern behind the Traffic LPV problem: too broad an audience, too little repetition, almost no conversion — a decision-heavy product (trip/experience) needs more than one touchpoint before purchase.

Caveat: this is average monthly frequency accumulated per ad, not a proven causal sequence — read it as a direction, not a fixed rule.
Part 7

General learnings

What worked

  1. Well-segmented Sales campaigns (Adv+ Broad, Retargeting, Mothers, Lookalikes) deliver CPA €23–45 on both platforms — the real engine of the month.
  2. Creator/partnership content remains the most consistent format when tested against the right audience.
  3. "1-word review"-style UGC performed very well at scale on TikTok.

What didn't work

  1. Traffic/LPV is the biggest offender of the month on both platforms. Meta: €17,461 (25% of budget) with a sales CPA of €256–293. TikTok: €8,616 with CPA €783 — the worst number in the account.
  2. The same creative/creator can do very well with one audience and very poorly with another — segmentation matters more than the creative in isolation.
  3. Pure Engagement/Reach doesn't contribute to CPA% — fine as support, but shouldn't be read against the sales metric.
