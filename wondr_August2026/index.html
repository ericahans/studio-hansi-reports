Sample size for bands above 2.5 is small (2–6 ads) — noisy, read as a direction rather than a fixed rule.

Why it matters Same 1.5-impressions-per-person threshold we saw on Barbie: below it, WONDR barely sells (CPA €111, nearly 3x worse than average). Crossing 1.5, CPA drops to €42 and conversion jumps ~8x. Unlike Barbie, here the pattern keeps improving as frequency climbs instead of leveling off — a sign the WONDR audience tolerates more message repetition before saturating (a more recurring/family product vs. a one-off travel decision).

Caveat: this is average monthly frequency accumulated per ad, not a proven causal sequence — read it as a direction, not a fixed rule.
Part 7

General learnings

What worked

  1. Creator UGC (@Maik Siekema, @Amy Kai) is the most efficient creative of the month on both platforms — CPA €15–24, well below the account average.
  2. Retargeting/Website Visitors converts consistently and cheaply (€23–30) on both platforms — the efficiency engine of the month.
  3. Smart+ Amsterdam (TikTok) is the single largest audience of the month, with a healthy CPA (€37) even at high volume.

What didn't work

  1. The same creative/creator can swing from CPA €18 to €200+ depending on the audience pairing — even within the same Sales campaign, it's not just about spend volume.
  2. The "@andalila_ausfluege AD" creative generated zero sales despite €2,646 invested — inside an audience (DE Germany Adv+) that converts normally (106 sales, CPA €39.23 for the whole ad set). It was marked as deleted but kept running — likely a Meta bug; action: verify and report to support.
  3. Pure Engagement/Reach doesn't contribute to CPA% — fine as support, but shouldn't be read against the sales metric.
